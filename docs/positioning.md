# What Raven is

## The sentence

Raven is the self-hosted decision record between your customers, your team and your coding agents: evidence goes in, one signed decision comes out, and the agent does the work. The same sentence leads the [README](../README.md), and `tests/test_customer_text.py` checks that the two copies match. Every other section on this page states a boundary and points at the document that backs it. Where a behaviour is planned rather than shipped, this page says so.

## Who it is for

Raven is for engineering teams past product-market fit that already run coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex or Cursor. One engineering leader can approve a tool under the spend line, so a first install needs no procurement cycle. At these teams customer evidence lives in calls, email and Slack, and agents wait on decisions that nobody routed to the person who can make them. The first customers come from the founders' network. The self-hosted core lets an engineer run Raven on their own machine before any vendor trust exists ([your instance and your data](overview.md#your-instance-and-your-data)).

## What breaks today

An agent hits a judgment call in the middle of a task and either stops or guesses. The person who should decide is found by asking around. What a customer said in a call or a thread never reaches the engineer doing the work. When something is decided, nobody can show later who decided it or on which evidence, and the answer is not reused the next time the same question comes up.

## What Raven is not

Raven is not a meeting bot: transcripts are pasted or imported, the Vexa adapter is an administrator opt-in per meeting occurrence, enabling it does not join a meeting, and Raven does no speech-to-text of its own ([meeting adapters](meeting-adapters.md)). It is not a ticket tracker: it records the decisions inside a task, not the task list. It is not an agent that merges, deploys or emails customers: a signed move runs as private repository preparation only, a person signs before any move starts, and a browser task never pushes, merges or deploys ([Today](startup-os.md), [browser tasks](browser-tasks.md)). It is not a chat assistant in Slack: Slack carries a notification that links back to the decision, and the conversational mode is a separate opt-in that reads a reply back before anything is recorded ([Slack](slack.md)). It is not hosted memory: the decision graph lives on your server and the proof of each finished task is exportable ([change proof](change-proof.md)). It is not a proposer without evidence: with no model key, a proposal is unavailable and Today says so instead of inventing work ([Today](startup-os.md)).

## The asset

The asset is the signed, attributable record of every decision: the question an agent asked, who was routed and why, the answer, the signature bound to the exact scope, the evidence cited by record and version, and whether the answer was later corrected or reused ([how a task unfolds](overview.md#the-decisions-inside-a-task), [decision source revisions](decision-source-revisions.md), [change proof](change-proof.md)). Human-to-human text joins this record only by an explicit act: an import through `POST /api/records` or `bridge_import_record`, `record:` in a Slack channel the bot can see, or a meeting import ([record import contract](record-import-contract.md), [Slack](slack.md), [meeting adapters](meeting-adapters.md)). Every imported record is attested as shared with every reader of the workspace, so the corpus is one every workspace reader may see ([context sources](context-sources.md#the-shared-rule-only-what-every-reader-may-see)). Every record imported over HTTP or MCP carries a server-stamped access scope naming who attested and imported it, and the Add evidence form on Today pastes a customer message as a record attributed to the person who pasted it ([pasted evidence](context-sources.md#pasted-evidence-and-session-records)). Session records, when an administrator turns them on, leave one compact record per finished task: the decisions asked, who answered, what was reused, the files touched and the proof id, which background research can cite ([session records](context-sources.md#pasted-evidence-and-session-records)). The person who imported a record, or an administrator, withdraws it; dependents go to review and the audit history stays ([withdrawing a record](context-sources.md#pasted-evidence-and-session-records)).

## Honest boundaries

- Proposals need an API model backend; without one, Find opportunities reports that investigation is unavailable and that no proposal was fabricated ([Today](startup-os.md)). Without a model key, Today lists the open loops a person can still close: follow-ups nobody adopted, answers that need a second look, checkpoints due and evidence recorded since the last investigation ([Today](startup-os.md)).
- A coding host on a developer's machine is resumed, not started: the local supervisor resumes a session that has stopped and leaves a working or held session alone ([host adapters](host-adapters.md#resume-an-idle-coding-session-after-a-person-replies)).
- Dispatch of a signed move and browser tasks need the workbench configured on the server; every workbench route answers 503 until browser execution is enabled ([browser tasks](browser-tasks.md), [reference](reference.md#rest-interface)).
- Codex native execution is experimental, qualified only for one exact binary version and model, and checked by an advisory CI job; it stays experimental until it is requalified at head ([execution harnesses](execution-harnesses.md)).
- Routing accuracy is measured by the replay bench, with targets of warm top-1 at or above 70 percent and wrong-person at or below 1 percent ([quality rubric, dimension 4](quality-rubric.md#4-routing-who-gets-asked)); the documentation publishes no number for the current head, and the full held-out run has not been repeated since the merge-weighting smoke ([the routing replay bench](reference.md#the-routing-replay-bench)).

## For startups

The pilot is one loop: import a customer conversation as a meeting transcript or a record, and with a model key set, Find opportunities returns one bounded proposal with its evidence cited by record and version. A person signs it in the inbox, and the signed move prepares a private copy of the repository on the workbench, where the resulting patch is reviewed in your normal code-review flow ([Today](startup-os.md), [browser tasks](browser-tasks.md)). Without a model key, the first week still gives routed questions, signed answers and reuse of earlier answers through the coding agent you already use ([run one useful task together](guided-pilot.md), [PILOT.md](../PILOT.md)). Prices are not stated in the documentation; the [license](../LICENSE) sets out the noncommercial terms and the commercial trial.
